# Evidence-based strategic review

Use at a consequential research decision, not for every edit or failed attempt.
The outer loop asks whether the problem and route still merit the next investment;
the inner loop proves, implements, tests and writes within that route. Passing an
inner-loop check does not establish research value. Reuse the current design,
idea and review records; this reference adds no new controller, score or routine
review gate.

## Keep the outcome stable, not an untested route

Recover the authorized question, intended contribution and actual acceptance
criteria. A target journal is an aspiration, not a guaranteed terminal state or
a model-score threshold. Translate it into project-specific, inspectable reasons
for investment: an important unresolved question, a substantive distinction from
the strongest applicable alternatives, and evidence capable of supporting the
claimed result. Do not invent a top-journal requirement when none was requested.

Separate author-locked objectives and scientific invariants from provisional
estimators, calibrations, proof routes and contribution hypotheses. A design
freeze fixes the baseline for interpretable comparison; it does not oblige the
project to defend a defeated route. Do not weaken the question, add unavailable
information, strengthen assumptions or change the outcome measure silently to
save an idea. Record the cost and scope of a proposed change before adopting it.

## Review at decision-changing boundaries

Review before the first substantial commitment to long proof development,
production computation or full manuscript expansion, using the idea specialist's
reconstructed strongest known solution and actual central probe. A literature
summary and intended future comparison cannot support that commitment. Bounded
discovery may proceed to obtain this evidence. Review again after a new close
neighbor, decisive derivation, valid contrast
or counterexample materially changes the case; or when a bounded phase ends
without reducing the main uncertainty. One event can cover several boundaries.
Do not defer correcting an invalid claim until the next checkpoint.

Reuse an existing applicable design, mentor or independent review rather than
add another ceremony. On resume, inspect unresolved triggers and changed evidence;
elapsed time or a new session alone is not a reason to restart selection. Stable
value evidence need not be repeatedly re-proved. A scoped edit still delivers its
requested artifact; it does not automatically become a project-wide strategy audit.

At the boundary, answer only what can change the next decision:

- What useful result remains beyond the strongest applicable neighbor or simple
  conventional solution, and why does it matter to this question? Novelty,
  correctness and value do not compensate for one another in an overall score.
  An insight, identification result, impossibility or boundary can be valuable
  without a new algorithm or a fabricated application. Explain the consequence
  of the actual remaining result: which meaningful uncertainty, limitation or
  distinction it resolves beyond the reconstructed known solution. Do not infer
  that consequence from technical difficulty or a target venue's reputation.
- What exact evidence supports or opposes that case now? Link source versions
  and locators, derivations, actual outputs and unresolved comparisons. Align
  information, assumptions and the relevant guarantees; power comparisons need
  valid size/calibration, and cost comparisons need comparable accuracy.
- If starting today with these results, would this be the best justified use of
  the next available effort? Ignore sunk cost. Compare materially different
  routes when there is a live reason to do so, including using the conventional
  method without inventing a new one. Do not impose candidate quotas or reopen
  every previously rejected route.
- Which next check can distinguish continue from pivot or stop, within the
  available phase budget? State the possible decision-changing outcomes before
  observing them. Use an actual resource bound or a concrete bounded work unit,
  not invented probabilities, prices or a universal iteration limit.

Early evidence need not settle the whole theorem before proof work is justified.
For a genuinely difficult theory question, a bounded investment in its first
load-bearing bridge may itself be the discriminating check. Prefer the cheapest
adequate check, not the easiest peripheral success. Where no cheap check exists,
say so and justify the bounded commitment rather than manufacture a pilot result.

## Decide at the scope the evidence supports

| Decision | Basis and next action |
| --- | --- |
| Continue | The problem and remaining contribution retain an evidenced case, no known fatal objection is concealed, and the next bounded action can resolve a material uncertainty or deliver the required result. State what remains provisional; continuation is not certification. |
| Pivot route | Evidence favors a materially different mechanism or proof/design route serving the same authorized objective. Preserve the old claim and failures, compare the relevant alternative, and reopen only changed dependencies. A new title or added complexity is not a pivot. |
| Park | A decisive input is unavailable, the phase budget is exhausted without resolution, or no currently justified check can change the decision. Preserve the unresolved question and an evidence-based reopen condition; do not label it false or expand the budget silently. |
| Stop route | The scoped claim is refuted, subsumed, or has no substantive remaining value after checking what the adverse evidence actually defeats. Stop its dependent investment and preserve valid results and negative evidence. Do not infer that every variant or the broader question is dead. |

Distinguish an execution defect, missing evidence, failed proof route, valid
counterexample and inadequate contribution using [goal-and-learning.md](goal-and-learning.md).
Difficulty, model inability, one failed route, a noisy pilot or a low reviewer
score is not enough to reject the scientific question. Conversely, a correct
theorem or a polished manuscript does not rescue an insignificant contribution.

Adapt same-objective routes within existing authority. A material change to the
author's question, acceptance criteria or resource commitment needs the author's
decision unless already authorized. Prepare the evidence and concrete choices;
continue unaffected authorized work. If no viable route remains in scope,
report that outcome rather than endlessly repairing or silently lowering the goal.

## Preserve decisions without adding process burden

Retain the first substantial-investment decision and its supporting evidence at
the existing candidate/design/review locator. Later decisions state the affected
scope, decisive evidence and next bounded check without overwriting that history.
Distinguish a genuinely new source/result from an originally omitted comparison,
a correction to a prior derivation, or a changed value criterion. If a limitation
was already known, explain why its earlier treatment changes rather than calling
it new evidence. Keep the original objective, conditions and strongest baseline
visible; a pure-theory case cannot silently become an implementation-value case.
Reuse linked records and omit facts already clear there. Preserve adverse results
and evidence-based reopen conditions; newer text is not inherently better.
No new mandatory file, state schema, dashboard, recurring meeting or reviewer panel.

When using the optional portfolio, keep its existing statuses: continuation uses
the applicable active/selected entry, unresolved parking uses `parked`, and a
rejected claim uses `rejected`. A substantive new candidate links its predecessor.
An alternative proof route within the same candidate needs a route note, not a
new candidate ID. Update the authoritative record before affected sidecar bindings.
Do not force a schema migration or treat the validator as a value judgment.

During an activated single-issue revision cycle, record `strategy-hold` with
`decision: park` or `stop-route`, the affected issue (or null for the route), a
reason, current evidence snapshots and an evidence-based `reopen_if`. This uses
the existing controller and design/issue authority; see
[continuous-revision.md](continuous-revision.md#strategic-hold-without-an-author-gate).
It preserves unresolved issues and blocks the cycle's selection, execution and
acceptance transitions without inventing an author gate. It does not authorize
parallel edits to unrelated issues. Do not mark the old issue closed, reset the
baseline or loosen its contract merely to move on.

When the user's requested selection standard requires independent assessment,
or a disputed high-impact investment decision warrants one, reuse the idea
specialist's applicable fresh assessment or obtain the missing scoped review.
Do not introduce it for every brief or bounded exploration. Give it the
original question, reconstructed strongest known solution, candidate's actual
increment and costs, source locators, probe evidence and unresolved findings.
Withhold the executor's intended verdict, prior PASS labels and manuscript page
count; they cannot establish contribution value. Ask what the increment changes
and whether its importance supports the proposed commitment, separately from
correctness. The same assessment can serve discovery and design; it is not a
second panel. Later fresh review serves a concrete disputed or changed decision,
not every checkpoint. Self-review is not independent review; model agreement is
not independent primary evidence.

This mechanism supports inspectable allocation and earlier correction. Only
real-task comparisons could establish improved research quality or reduced rework;
structural checks, synthetic scenarios and review agreement cannot establish
convergence to a publishable result or guarantee journal acceptance.
