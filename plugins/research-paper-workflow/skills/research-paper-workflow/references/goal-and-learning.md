# Goal-directed work and evidence-checked experience

Read when prioritizing a long/resumed project, reusing project experience, or
correcting a repeated failure. Apply the relevant parts, not a new compulsory
pipeline. Use existing notes, decisions, proof/result records and dependency
links; a small edit or read-only request needs no new state.

## Work backward from the requested outcome

Recover the current authorized objective and acceptance criterion from the
request and latest effective author decisions. Identify the unresolved result,
comparison, implementation or explanation that most directly blocks delivery.
In an existing revision cycle, respect its admitted scope and dependency order;
do not bypass the controller to chase a more attractive task.

Choose the next useful action by its effect on that bottleneck. Prefer fixing
an invalid main claim, closing a load-bearing bridge, or obtaining discriminating
evidence over accumulating peripheral cards, searches, reports or cosmetic edits.
An early investment may be worthwhile when it prevents expensive downstream
failure. Use a qualitative reason, not invented success probabilities or scores.
Honor an explicitly requested local task even when a larger project is unfinished.

Keep a compact working brief in existing notes when persistence is useful:
**target and acceptance; current bottleneck; next action and what it resolves;
evidence/decision locators; stop, switch or escalation condition**. Omit fields
that add nothing. This is navigation, not another claims or issue database.

At a substantive result or a failed attempt, ask whether the bottleneck changed.
An inconclusive attempt is not progress toward proof merely because it generated
files. Change the diagnostic or route when new evidence warrants it; repeating
the same failed action without new information is not iteration. Do not quietly
weaken the target, remove required evidence or promote a pilot to completion.
When the requested outcome and necessary checks are met, deliver and stop.

At a consequential investment boundary, distinguish progress on the current
route from progress toward a worthwhile contribution. Use
[strategic-review.md](strategic-review.md) when new evidence or an uninformative
bounded phase changes the case for further investment. An important question
can survive a failed route; an insignificant contribution does not become
important through more complete proofs. Keep the authorized objective stable,
but allow evidence-backed route changes instead of treating design freeze or
sunk effort as a reason to continue. Reuse this same working brief.

## Reuse experience at its actual evidence level

Separate **what the note says** (fact, local observation, tentative explanation,
or conditional reusable guidance) from **how well it is supported**. A successful
run, confident summary or repeated retrieval does not validate a general rule.
One failure does not justify a permanent ban on a method.

For an experience that will affect a decision, recover from existing records:
its original source/version or actual attempt, the conditions where it applies,
the check supporting it, and any known counterexample or superseding decision.
Missing support means tentative, not false. Unchanged, checked evidence within
the same scope can be reused without a new experiment or review. A local
observation may guide a bounded probe but cannot settle a scientific claim.
An established fact is established only within the specialist evidence's scope;
neither a memory label nor a file hash proves it.

A mentor/style card, generated digest or matched quotation is a retrieval aid,
not independent support. Before it changes a scientific decision, check the
underlying passage's attribution, context, conditions and actual implication.
Multiple derivatives of one source or run do not create additional independent
primary evidence. Independently performed checks of that same artifact may
still constitute independent review. Reuse an applicable source check rather
than reopening it merely because the card was retrieved again.

Before generalizing an experience, use the cheapest adequate discriminating
check: reopen the exact passage, reproduce the implicated behavior, derive the
condition, or test a relevant contrast/counterexample. Check a proposed causal
explanation against plausible tool, parser, configuration, version or selection
effects where material. If evidence cannot distinguish them, retain uncertainty.
Do not demand every kind of check or another mentor review for each note.

## Correct errors without restarting the project

### Diagnose before choosing repair or pivot

For a consequential failure, locate the first unsupported link and choose the
smallest check that can change the next decision. This is a reasoning guide,
not a mandatory classification form:

| What failed | What follows |
| --- | --- |
| Access, execution, parsing, indexing, solver or measurement | Repair or diagnose that path; the scientific hypothesis remains undecided. A corrected runner does not validate old outputs. |
| Evidence coverage, precision, aggregation or source applicability | Reconcile the actual units, conditions, denominators, missing/failed outcomes and source scope; obtain discriminating evidence or retain an explicit gap. A matching number alone does not support the claim. |
| A proof route or auxiliary assumption | Recheck the obligation and source conditions or try a justified route; failure to prove is not a counterexample. |
| A valid contrast or counterexample under the claimed conditions | Revise/reject the affected claim or propose a justified design change, preserving the negative result and actual scope. Do not silently replace the author's target. |

When causes remain confounded, keep them unresolved and run a diagnostic that
separates them; do not invent a root cause or pivot because a reviewer score
fell. Repeated attempts warrant a new route only when they add information or
address a different evidenced cause. A material target/budget change still
requires the author's decision; unchanged authorized work proceeds normally.

When credible contrary evidence appears, stop relying on the disputed experience
for the affected action. Distinguish a contradiction from an out-of-scope use;
do not erase a valid conditional result because its conditions no longer hold.
Mark the experience as contested while unresolved, or refuted/superseded when
the evidence warrants that status. Preserve the original wording and source;
append the correction, reason, scope and replacement locator in the same owner
record. More recent wording alone does not override an author decision.

Follow actual uses/dependencies to the affected proofs, code, results and prose.
Identify which consumers need checking, which have been corrected, and which
remain pending; if uses are unknown, report that gap rather than claim full
closure. Stop stale guidance from appearing as current in the active summary.
Recheck only the affected closure with its specialist; leave unrelated valid
work alone. Correcting a rule does not retroactively validate its old consumers.
Keep historical failures and superseded guidance for audit, not as active advice.

At a natural handoff, retain experiences that change future decisions with their
scope and locators. Do not repeatedly rewrite stable notes or copy every attempt
into global instructions. Remove obsolete guidance from the active brief without
deleting its history. Global skill promotion remains requested maintenance under
the manager's improvement loop, not an automatic consequence of finishing a paper.

## Spend process effort where it changes the outcome

Before adding a report, controller, reviewer or check, identify the concrete
failure it would prevent and whether existing evidence already covers it.
Combine overlapping checks at a stable boundary; reuse current support when
object, assumptions, inputs and implementation remain applicable. Do not relax
an existing scientific or activated release requirement for convenience.

For a low-impact edit, implement the requested artifact and inspect the affected
content directly. Do not initialize continuous revision, make another issue
database, or request a full scientific review. For a load-bearing scientific
change, preserve the specialist's proof/evidence and independent-review duties.
An explicitly activated continuous cycle retains its frozen report/review gates;
ordinary work does not inherit those gates merely because it is called "final".

Use the existing exploration loop for bounded uncertain probes: decide what the
probe can discriminate and when to stop before spending resources. Exploration
may fail; final scientific claims still require their full evidence. Persist a
running job's handle and wait for dependent results rather than draft around a
guessed outcome. Supply workers the current brief and necessary original
artifacts, not the entire history or summaries of summaries.

When evaluating workflow changes, measure completion of the same substantive
task, independent substantive errors, avoidable rework and observed time/cost.
Card counts, green checks, shorter prompts and self-reported confidence are not
substitutes. Reuse the manager's comparison tools when a comparison is actually
requested; do not add a benchmark to every research task. Unknown usage stays
unknown, and synthetic checks do not establish general research-quality gains.

For authorized prompt or model adaptation, use [prompt-trials.md](prompt-trials.md)
and the existing manager's frozen evaluations. A successful comparison command
does not mean adoption: ties keep the baseline. Select only current, evidenced
model/task profiles; do not add this process to ordinary paper work or promote
a local answer correction into a permanent rule.

### Review the artifact, then verify the repair

At first-pass scientific judgment, supply the question, applicable criteria and
direct access to the scoped original evidence, not the executor's preferred
interpretation or a desired score. For repair verification, also supply the
original finding and acceptance criterion: check the old failure against the
current changed artifact and its affected consumers before reading a persuasive
response. Preserve unresolved findings across fresh reviews; an explicit,
evidence-backed rejection can close a mistaken finding. Use
[evidence-first-review.md](evidence-first-review.md) for its existing material
re-review scope; do not activate its full packet sequence for every small fix.

Admit a proposed fix by its concrete artifact/condition, consequence for the
requested result and evidence that it addresses the problem. Keep hypothetical
hardening, extra templates and preferences outside the active queue unless they
protect an applicable requirement or the user requested them. Do not demand a
new defect when the artifact is already adequate. Batch overlapping checks once
the relevant changes stabilize; keep required scientific reviews intact and
stop when the requested outcome and its checks are met, not at a score threshold.

## Basis and boundary

[SpireBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.12596v1), especially Sections 4–5 and
Appendix H.6, motivates examining delayed outcomes and unchecked experience.
The research-specific practices above are a design adaptation, not effects
demonstrated by its game experiments. Behavioral caution in a game does not
establish a cause of defensive academic prose.

The [2026-10-15 upstream comparison](upstream-evidence.md#2026-10-15-source-level-reinspection-and-selective-integration)
adds source-level design reasons for failure diagnosis, evidence-grounded review
and limited process admission. Those external evaluations do not measure this
workflow's scientific quality or efficiency.
